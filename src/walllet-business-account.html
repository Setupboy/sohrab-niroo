Phase A · steps 1–2

Consent & entry.

Accept Bridge’s terms and open the Persona verification flow.

Phase A · Consent & entry compliance.bridge.xyz
01

Step 1. Accept the Bridge terms

The journey starts inside Bridge, the platform that actually opens the account. Before it can hand you to its verification vendor you have to accept its Terms of Service and Privacy Policy, after which it redirects you automatically.

What you do here

  • Read the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy 1
  • Tap Accept
  • A green confirmation appears 2 and Bridge redirects you

Watch out

Good to know Persona is only the verification vendor. Bridge is the company you are onboarding with — Persona collects and checks the evidence on its behalf.
Tip If the page seems to hang after accepting, do not open a second tab. Reload this one; Bridge re-uses the same inquiry.
Phase A · Consent & entry bridge.withpersona.com
02

Step 2. Persona opens — read the warning

You are now on bridge.withpersona.com, the Persona-hosted business verification. The bold warning on this screen is the single most important sentence in the whole flow.

What you do here

  • Tap Get started

Watch out

Watch out. Progress is not saved. Close the tab before finishing and you re-enter everything from scratch. Budget 30–60 uninterrupted minutes.
Tip Gather first: registration letter, incorporation date, registration number, full registered address, a 200+ character business description, and PDFs for proof of address and good standing.
B

Phase B · steps 3–6

Business details.

Tell Persona who the legal entity is, what it does and who owns it.

Phase B · Business details Business registration
03

Step 3. Where is your business registered?

The first real question sets the rule-set for everything after it. The country of registration decides which documents Persona asks for later and whether you get EEA-specific questions.

What you do here

  • Select the country where the entity is legally registered
  • Answer Does your business primarily operate in a different country? — here: No

Watch out

Tip Answer Yes only if day-to-day operations genuinely sit elsewhere — it adds an operating-country branch and usually more documents.
Good to know This country is locked into the address fields further down, so you cannot silently contradict yourself later.
Phase B · Business details Business profile
04

Step 4. Fill in the business identity block

The first full form. Everything here is cross-checked against the documents you upload in Phase C, so copy the values off the registration letter rather than from memory.

What you do here

  • Business legal name — exactly as on the certificate
  • Business entity type 1 — here: Sole proprietorship
  • Incorporation date — month / day / year
  • DBA / trade name — optional
  • Email address and phone number
  • Business website — or tick No website
  • Business registration number 2

Watch out

Watch out. Strip the dots from an Armenian registration number. Armenian certificates print it grouped — 123.456.7890 — but the field expects digits only: 1234567890. Leave the dots out or the number will not match the registry.
Tip For a sole proprietorship / IE the legal name is usually the owner’s own full name, not a brand name. The brand goes in the DBA field.
Watch out. Otherwise the registration number and legal name must match the certificate character-for-character, including leading zeros. A mismatch here is the most common cause of manual review.
Tip No website is fine — tick the box. The proof of business activity row in Phase C is satisfied by your registration letter either way (Step 8).
Phase B · Business details Business profile
05

Step 5. Industry, description and addresses

The second half of the business form — and where nearly everyone gets bounced back. Two of its rules are never shown until you tap Continue.

What you do here

  • Business industry (NAICS code) 1 — closest match, only one
  • Business description — free text, 200 character minimum
  • Business registered address — no P.O. boxes
  • Business operating address — tick Same as Registered Address if identical
  • Is your entity a DAO? — Yes / No

Watch out

Watch out. Hidden rule 1 — only one industry. The field behaves like a multi-select and accepts several tags, then rejects them 2 with ‘The maximum allowed number of choices is 1’. Remove the extras with the small ×.
Tip The NAICS code does not have to be an exact match. It is a broad classification, not a legal declaration — pick the closest of the options below and let the description carry the specifics.
Watch out. Hidden rule 2 — 200 character minimum on the description 2. The text in the screenshot is only 160 characters, which is exactly why it came back red. Aim for 250+ so you have margin.
Tip Let an AI write the description. Paste your CV or a few bullet points into any AI assistant with the prompt below, then paste the result back. Draft it outside the form — the session is not saved, so you do not want to be composing text in a live field.
Good to know The country row of the address is greyed out — inherited from the registration country in Step 3.

Write a business description for a KYB verification form. Requirements: at least 250 characters, one paragraph, plain factual English with no marketing language. Say what services the business sells, who the customers are, and how it earns revenue.
Phase B · Business details Your information & UBOs
06

Step 6. Declare the owners, then attest

Persona now asks what you are to the business, then collects every other owner, signer and control person. The three definitions on the first screen are regulatory, not cosmetic.

What you do here

  • First name, last name, title 1
  • Beneficial owner — at least 25% ownership
  • Control person — controls, manages or directs the business
  • Signer — can authorise transactions
  • Email for the onboarding link, country of residence
  • Tick the attestation 2 and continue

Watch out

Tip Sole proprietorship / IE: there is only one owner, and it is you. An individual entrepreneur cannot have co-owners, so tick all three roles, add nobody else, and the UBO screen will show a single Associated Person 1 (you).
Tip LLC: usually one owner too. Unless you actually brought in partners, the LLC is you at 100%. Only add people here if they genuinely hold 25%+ or control the business.
Watch out. At least one signer is required across the whole application. If every signer box is left unticked the flow cannot complete.
Watch out. The attestation legally certifies that the ownership information is complete and correct. Omitting a 25%+ owner is a compliance failure, not a form typo.
Good to know Everyone you add here receives their own verification email and must finish their own KYC (Phase E) before the business can be approved. Warn them in advance.

Phase C · steps 7–12

Business documents.

Upload the evidence pack: registration, address, good standing, tax.

Phase C · Business documents Required submissions
07

Step 7. The document checklist appears

This hub is the spine of Phase C. Each row opens a sub-flow and turns into a check mark when done; Submit documents stays greyed out until every row is complete.

What you do here

  • UBO Information — already complete
  • Proof of business activity
  • Proof of address
  • Evidence of good standing
  • EEA Required Information

Watch out

Good to know The exact rows depend on your registration country — the EEA row appears because of the country chosen in Step 3.
Tip Take the rows in any order. Start with whichever document you already have open.
Phase C · Business documents Proof of business activity
08

Step 8. Upload the company registration letter

This row wants proof that the entity legally exists and trades. The document to use is the company registration letter — the certificate the registry issued when the business was incorporated.

What you do here

  • Locate the registration letter from the company registry
  • Upload the file
  • Wait for the row to show a check mark

Watch out

Tip Use the original digital PDF, not a photo of it. Find the registration letter in the email the registry sent when the company was registered and upload that file directly. The original keeps its text layer and digital signature, so the OCR reads every field cleanly. Phone photos and re-scans of printouts are where rejections come from.
Watch out. Do not confuse this row with Evidence of good standing further down. The registration letter belongs here; good standing takes a separate document (Step 10).
Phase C · Business documents Proof of Address
09

Step 9. Upload the business proof of address

The requirements are spelled out precisely and the OCR checks them: the document must be dated within the last 3 months and clearly show the date, the business name and the operating address.

What you do here

  • Tap + to attach a file or photo 1
  • Tap Submit and wait while it is read 2
  • The row turns green on the checklist 3

Watch out

Tip In Armenia, go to your bank for this one. Utility and telecom bills are rarely issued in a company’s name here, so the two documents that reliably clear this row are a bank statement that prints the address, or a letter of reference from the bank — both drawn on the business account.
Watch out. Check the statement actually shows the address before uploading. Many Armenian statements print the account holder and the period but omit the address, which fails this row on the spot. Ask the branch for a version that includes it, or request the reference letter instead.
Watch out. A document older than 3 months is auto-rejected even if everything else is perfect — a reference letter should be freshly issued.
Tip Remote or home-based business? Persona explicitly allows the residential address of a control person as the address of actual operations.
Phase C · Business documents Evidence of Good Standing
10

Step 10. Prove the entity is active

A supplementary document showing the business is active and in good standing. Your registration letter has already been used one row up, so this row takes something else — and a business bank statement showing some transactions does the job.

What you do here

  • Attach a PDF or clear photos 1
  • An accepted example looks like 2
  • Tap Submit and wait for processing 3

Watch out

Tip A business bank statement with some transactions on it is accepted here. The transactions are the point — they evidence that the account is live and the business is actually trading. Pick a period with real activity rather than a quiet month. A certificate of good standing or a registry extract works too.
Watch out. Do not crop the transaction rows out. They are what proves the account is active, so include the pages that show them rather than just the header page.
Good to know The same statement can serve this row and proof of address (Step 9), so one PDF from online banking may cover both — provided it prints the address, which that row requires and this one does not.
Tip Checklist for any upload: issuer visible, business name visible, date inside the window, transactions or address visible as the row requires, nothing cropped, all four corners in frame.
Phase C · Business documents EEA Required Information
11

Step 11. Foreign tax registration

The final row is a single regulatory question driven by European rules: are you registered with any foreign tax authorities outside your incorporation country?

What you do here

  • Open EEA Required Information, the last open row 1
  • Answer Yes or No 2 — here: No
  • Tap Continue

Watch out

Watch out. Answer Yes and you are asked for each foreign tax jurisdiction and its tax identification number, so have those ready before tapping.
Good to know This mirrors CRS / FATCA-style tax-residency reporting.
Phase C · Business documents Required submissions
12

Step 12. Submit the document pack

Every row is green and Submit documents is finally active. Tapping it locks the business file and hands it to compliance.

What you do here

  • Tap Submit documents

Watch out

Watch out. After this you cannot edit the business answers from inside the flow. Re-check the entity name and registration number before tapping.

Funds & risk profile.

Explain where money comes from and how the account will be used.

Phase D · Funds & risk profile Next steps
13

Step 13. What happens next, explained

A bridging screen with two messages: emails have gone out to every beneficial owner you listed, and because you listed yourself you will get one too. One section still remains in this session.

What you do here

  • Tap Next to continue to the source-of-funds section

Watch out

Good to know Two tracks now run in parallel: you finish the funds questionnaire here, and every listed person completes their own KYC by email (Phase E).
Tip The business is not verified until the last person on the UBO list finishes their email link. With a single-owner IE or LLC, that person is just you.
Phase D · Funds & risk profile Source of funds
14

Step 14. Where the money comes from

The financial-crime questionnaire. Persona wants a coherent story: the source category, a narrative, an expected size, and whether you move other people’s money.

What you do here

  • Primary source of funds — here: Sales of Goods and Services
  • Source of funds description — narrative free text
  • Estimated annual revenue — here: $0 – $99,999 USD
  • Transmitting funds on behalf of customers — here: Not applicable. We will only move internal funds

Watch out

Watch out. The last question is the important one. ‘Yes’ means you act as a money transmitter for third parties and triggers a far heavier review, including proof of your own KYC/KYB/AML programme. Pick ‘Not applicable’ only if that is genuinely true.
Tip Keep this description consistent with the business description from Step 5 and with the revenue band you choose.
Phase D · Funds & risk profile Business activity
15

Step 15. Restricted countries and high-risk activities

Two screening questions: whether you operate in comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions, then a checklist of regulated and high-risk activities.

What you do here

  • Sanctioned countries — Cuba, Iran, Myanmar, North Korea, Syria — here: No
  • Tick every activity that applies — here: None of the above

Watch out

Good to know The list covers money services (check cashing, gift cards, ATMs, remittances), lending/banking, foreign exchange & virtual-currency brokerage/OTC, holding client funds (escrow), investment services, safe deposit boxes, marijuana, third-party payment processing, adult entertainment, weapons, gambling, nicotine/tobacco, pharmaceuticals, precious metals and stones.
Watch out. Under-declaring here is the fastest route to an account being frozen later. If an activity is even partly yours, tick it and explain.
Phase D · Funds & risk profile Expected account usage
16

Step 16. How the account will be used

The last business question. Persona is establishing a baseline so unusual activity can be detected later. Submitting it closes the session that started at Step 1.

What you do here

  • Primary account purpose — here: Receive payment for goods and services
  • Expected monthly send + receive volume — here: US$3,000
  • Tap Submit 1 — the business half is done 2

Watch out

Tip Give a realistic monthly figure. Wildly exceeding your stated volume later triggers monitoring alerts; understating it to look safe works against you.
Good to know Nothing is approved yet. The file is decided only once every listed individual finishes Phase E.

Phase E · steps 17–25

Individual verification.

Each owner / control person proves who they are (KYC).

Phase E · Individual verification Email
17

Step 17. The individual verification email

Persona Identities, Inc. emails every person you listed. The message names the business, gives a deadline and a single Verify identity button.

What you do here

  • Open the email on a phone with a working camera
  • Tap Verify identity

Watch out

Watch out. The link expires in 7 days. After that the person must be re-invited.
Tip Do this on a phone, not a laptop — the ID capture needs a real camera. Check spam if it has not arrived within a few minutes.
Phase E · Individual verification Getting started
18

Step 18. Biometric consent and the personal checklist

The individual (KYC) inquiry opens with the same ‘single session’ warning plus a biometric consent notice: Persona will process biometric information from your face and ID to verify identity and detect fraud.

What you do here

  • Read the biometric notice and privacy policy 1
  • Optionally switch language at the bottom
  • Consent, and the personal checklist opens 2

Watch out

Good to know Persona states biometric information is stored for no more than 3 years.
Watch out. This session is not saved either. Have the physical ID document in hand before you tap.
Tip Rows unlock progressively — do not assume the three rows you see here are the final list.
Phase E · Individual verification Country
19

Step 19. Where do you live?

Your country of residence determines which government IDs are accepted and whether extra documents are required.

What you do here

  • Select your country of residence
  • Tap Select

Watch out

Tip This is residence — not nationality, and not the business registration country. All three can differ.
Phase E · Individual verification Basic Information
20

Step 20. Your personal details

Straightforward identity fields — but every one of them must match the ID document you are about to photograph.

What you do here

  • First / middle / last name 1
  • Birthdate
  • Email address and phone number
  • National Identification Number
  • Home address: street, unit, city, postcode 2

Watch out

Watch out. National Identification Number means your residency number. If you hold a residence card, enter the number printed on that card — the AN… number — not a passport number and not a national ID from another country.
Watch out. Spell the name exactly as printed on the ID, including transliteration. ‘Mohammad’ against ‘Muhammad’ is enough to fail the automated match.
Tip The identification number field is masked — tap the eye icon and re-read it before continuing.
Tip Use the address that appears on the proof-of-address document you will upload in Step 23.
Phase E · Individual verification Government ID
21

Step 21. Which ID are you using?

You pick the issuing country, and Persona then offers the ID types it can read for it — passport, national ID card, residence card or driving licence.

What you do here

  • Select the country that issued your ID
  • Tap Select

Watch out

Tip A passport is a single-page capture and generally the smoothest. Cards require both a front and a back photo — see the next step for why the back is the hard part.
Phase E · Individual verification Government ID
22

Step 22. Photograph your ID

Camera capture with a framing guide and a live warning to check lighting and lettering before continuing. Cards need both sides; the back is where most people get stuck.

What you do here

  • Photograph the front, review, Use this photo 1
  • Photograph the back and confirm 2
  • Wait through the upload 3 and processing 4

Watch out

Watch out. It hunts for a barcode your card does not have. Armenian residence cards carry no barcode, so the back capture 2 can fail again and again with no useful error. There is no way to skip it — the workaround is to keep retrying while changing the conditions: different lighting, a different background surface, a slightly different angle and distance. It usually takes several attempts before a frame is accepted.
Watch out. The photo must be taken live. Do not photograph a printout, hold a scan up on another screen, or upload a saved image file — Persona detects these and rejects them. Capture the physical document with the phone camera in the moment.
Tip Kill the glare: matte surface, no direct overhead light, no flash on laminate, phone held parallel to the card rather than at an angle.
Watch out. If any corner is cut off, retake it. Cropped edges are a common auto-rejection, and backgrounding the browser mid-upload can drop the whole session.
Good to know A selfie / liveness check can follow here, matching your face to the ID portrait. That is what the biometric consent in Step 18 covered.
Phase E · Individual verification Proof of address
23

Step 23. Your personal proof of address

After the ID step a new row appears. The rules are the same as the business version, applied to you: a document from the last 3 months showing the date, your name and your address.

What you do here

  • A new Proof of Address row appears 1
  • Attach the document 2
  • Tap Submit and wait for processing 3

Watch out

Tip The document that works is a personal bank statement or a bank reference letter that prints your address. Download the original PDF from online banking rather than photographing a printout, and confirm the address is actually on the page before uploading.
Watch out. The name must match the name on your ID, and the address must match what you typed in Step 20.
Good to know This is the personal counterpart of the business row in Step 9 — same three-month window, same OCR checks.
Phase E · Individual verification Ownership Info
24

Step 24. Confirm your role and stake

The personal mirror of the business-side roles screen, with two additions: your exact percentage ownership, and a director role.

What you do here

  • Your title — here: CEO
  • Beneficial owner + percentage — here: 100
  • Control person, signer, director
  • Tap Continue

Watch out

Tip For a single-owner IE or LLC this is simply 100, with all four boxes ticked — you are the owner, the control person, the signer and the director.
Watch out. With more than one owner, the percentages across everyone must be consistent with the UBO list you submitted in Step 6.
Good to know ‘Director’ appears only in the individual flow — it was not one of the three roles on the business-side screen.
Phase E · Individual verification Complete
25

Step 25. All rows green — submitted

Basic information, Government ID, Proof of Address and Ownership Info are complete. Continue submits the individual verification; Persona runs its checks and Bridge’s compliance team reviews the file, reaching out only if something else is needed.

What you do here

  • Tap Continue with all four rows green 1
  • The submission is confirmed 2

Watch out

Good to know Repeat Phase E for every person on the UBO list. With a single-owner IE or LLC you are the only one, and the file is now complete.
Tip Keep every document you uploaded. If compliance comes back with a question, it is almost always about a mismatch between one of them and the form.
